That's only Gate's side of the bill. Add a foreign transaction fee if your card currency doesn't match the payment processor's, and a 3.5% platform fee can quietly become 7–8% of the money you spent.

## Route 2: P2P, where buyers pay Gate nothing

P2P (also labeled C2C in places) is a marketplace. You're buying from verified users and professional merchants, not from Gate directly. The seller's USDT gets locked in escrow the moment a trade opens, you pay by whatever method you and the merchant agreed on, and the escrow releases to your wallet when the seller confirms receipt.

Two things make it the default recommendation for anyone buying with local currency:

**Platform fees are zero for buyers.** The price you see is what you pay. Gate's guides state this plainly and contrast it with card purchases, where platform fees and bank charges stack.

**Payment methods are wide.** Bank transfers, PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Wise, Revolut, Skrill, NetEase-style local wallets, regional bank apps — more than 15 methods depending on where you are. If your bank blocks crypto card purchases, this is usually the workaround.

The cost doesn't disappear, it just moves somewhere less obvious: merchants build a small margin into their quoted rate, typically 0.5–1%. Because merchants compete with each other on the same marketplace, that margin stays compressed. You should still scan a few offers rather than taking the first one — a 30-second look at the order book is where the actual saving happens.

Transaction size on P2P is set by each merchant rather than by Gate, so minimums and maximums vary by offer. In practice you'll see offers starting in the $10–$50 range and going up to several thousand dollars, which is a much wider band than card purchases allow.

Step by step:

1. Complete KYC and turn on two-factor authentication — this is mandatory for P2P, not optional
2. Add at least one payment method to your P2P profile
3. Choose your local currency and the USDT pair
4. Browse offers, filtering by payment method and rate
5. Pick a merchant with a high completion rate and a real trade history — above 95% completion is a reasonable floor
6. Enter the amount, start the trade, and send payment within the merchant's time limit
7. Mark the payment as sent; escrow releases the USDT, usually within minutes

Most of the horror stories about P2P come from one mistake: paying outside the platform's order flow, or releasing funds before confirming. The escrow only protects you if the trade stays inside it.

## Route 3: If you already hold crypto, skip the fiat queue

Three options exist for people who aren't starting from zero:

**Convert.** One-click swap from another asset into USDT, with a small spread instead of a stated fee. Fast, but compare the quoted rate before moving size — spreads are where Convert makes its money.

**On-chain deposit.** Send USDT from an external wallet or another exchange. Gate charges no platform fee for crypto deposits, though the network you send on charges its own. Pick the chain deliberately: TRON and layer-2 networks are much cheaper than Ethereum mainnet for moving stablecoins, and the recipient network has to match the one you send on or the funds are effectively gone.

**GateCode.** A transfer code you can redeem between Gate users, with no fee and no address involved. Useful for moving value inside the platform without touching a chain.

## Before you hit buy

Four things that cause most failed or costly first purchases:

**KYC.** Required, done once, usually processed within 24 hours. Gate's own guides put it there, and P2P won't work without it. Higher verification levels unlock higher limits and more payment channels.

**Name matching.** The legal name on your card or bank account must match your verified account name exactly. This is a hard rule, not a preference.

**Security setup.** Two-factor authentication before your first trade, plus an anti-phishing code and a withdrawal address whitelist. For P2P specifically, 2FA is mandatory and you need at least one payment method registered.

**Region eligibility.** Gate restricts or prohibits all or part of its services in certain regions — the platform's own disclaimer names the US, Canada, Iran and Cuba among restricted areas, and card purchases are unavailable or limited in additional jurisdictions. Check before you deposit, not after.

## Mistakes that cost the most

**Using a credit card and calling it cheap.** On a $1,000 purchase in a high-fee region, the combined platform fee, cash advance fee and immediate interest can hit 6.5–8.5%. That's before USDT has done anything.

**Ignoring the 72-hour hold.** Crypto bought with a new card can be locked from withdrawal for three days. Tradable, not transferable.

**Taking the first P2P offer.** Merchants compete on the same order book. Comparing two or three offers is a 30-second activity that regularly saves more than any coupon.

**Sending USDT on the wrong network.** TRON and layer-2 networks are far cheaper than Ethereum mainnet for stablecoin transfers, and the network has to match on both ends. Small test transfer first, every time.

**Chasing reward thresholds with volume you didn't plan to trade.** The advanced track rewards scale with deposit size and cumulative volume. Trading more than you intended to unlock a coupon is a losing trade dressed up as a bonus.
